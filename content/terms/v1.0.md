## What we are agreeing to

C-Transit is building a business together. This agreement is how we record and compare the value of what each person contributes so that, when the time comes to formalize the business as a company, we have a shared, honest, auditable basis for determining relative ownership.

We agree to:

1. **Record contributions honestly.** The hours you log in Execution Tracker are hours you actually worked for C-Transit. The expenses you claim are expenses you actually incurred for C-Transit. The work you describe is work you actually did.

2. **Submit evidence.** Every hour and every expense should be capable of being supported by evidence if challenged — a git commit, a file, a receipt, a screenshot, a customer message, a design, a schematic. The platform records your submissions; the evidence exists in the real world.

3. **Accept the admin review.** Every session and every contribution is reviewed by an admin before it converts into equity credit. The admin decides whether it counts. You may disagree with a decision, but you cannot override it. The disagreement process is described below.

4. **Keep the ledger clean.** You will not attempt to log time you did not work, claim an expense you did not incur, log the same work twice, or use the platform in any way that misrepresents your contribution.

5. **Participate in good faith.** If you forget to log an entry, you will use the explanation path honestly. If you disagree with an admin's review, you will raise it openly. If you notice a problem with the ledger, you will report it rather than hide it.

## How contributions are valued

The system uses a **Slicing Pie**-style method. Three principles:

**Time and non-cash contributions earn credit at 2× their fair-market value.**
Your hourly contribution is valued at the agreed fair-market salary for your role, and that value is multiplied by 2 to become slices. If your FMV is ₦10,000 per hour, one hour of approved time earns 20,000 slices.

**Cash and unreimbursed expenses earn credit at 4× their value.**
If you spend ₦100,000 of your own money on approved business expenses without reimbursement, it becomes 400,000 slices. This higher multiplier reflects the fact that cash carries more risk than time.

**The multipliers may be adjusted by written agreement of all affected members.**
The default values above are not legal requirements — they are the starting rules this team has chosen. Changing them requires agreement, and changes only apply going forward.

The exact FMV for each role is documented in the Role & FMV Schedule you sign alongside your physical copy of these terms.

## How time is recorded and reviewed

Every hour of work is logged through a work session:

1. **You start a session** when you begin working. This records a timestamp.
2. **You submit entries** describing what you did. Each entry is a written record of a specific period of work. The system expects at least one entry per hour.
3. **The session continues** as long as your browser sends heartbeats every hour. If the browser goes silent for two hours, the session closes automatically.
4. **When the session ends**, the total approved minutes become a time log that waits for admin review.
5. **An admin reviews** the log. They either approve it, reject it with a specific reason, or flag it for further discussion.
6. **Only approved logs convert** into slices at the next payday run.

If a session ends and you did not submit enough entries, you will be prompted to explain — what you did, why you did not log an entry, and how long you actually worked. That explanation goes into the same review queue. The admin sees your words alongside your heartbeat history and decides.

**No log converts to slices without admin approval.** The admin is the arbiter. If your account is the only admin's account, that means the founder reviews the founder.

## What the ledger is, and is not

The ledger is a permanent record. Once a contribution has been entered and accepted, it is not edited. Corrections are new rows that reverse the effect of the original row. Both the original and the correction remain visible forever.

**The ledger is not a promise of cash.** FMV is a valuation benchmark, not a salary. If C-Transit has agreed to pay you a salary, that salary is a separate obligation and is unaffected by this ledger. No one is required to work unpaid, and no one may be asked to waive statutory wages. If you are an employee under Nigerian law, your statutory rights — minimum wage, pension, leave, notice, tax — are not affected by anything in this agreement.

**The ledger is not a share certificate.** Recording 200,000 slices does not make you a shareholder of any company. It is a contractual accounting record. If C-Transit later incorporates as a limited liability company, converting these recorded slices into actual shares requires separate legal documents, drafted by a lawyer and signed by all parties. Nothing in this agreement issues shares.

**The ledger is not an exit guarantee.** If you leave C-Transit, your accrued slices are handled according to the departure rules of the physical agreement. This app records the departure event; the physical agreement defines what happens to the equity.

## How confidential information is protected

C-Transit's source code, firmware, designs, schematics, customer data, partner terms, pricing, business plans, and financials are confidential.

You agree to:

- Use confidential information only for C-Transit work.
- Not upload company code, customer data, internal diagrams, or non-public plans to public AI assistants, public code hosts, or unapproved third-party services.
- Not store confidential material on personal devices or accounts except where explicitly authorized and secured.
- Report suspected leaks, stolen devices, or unauthorized access immediately — within 24 hours, using the contact channel your role schedule provides.
- Return all C-Transit devices, credentials, and materials when you leave, and confirm deletion of any local copies in writing.

General skills you acquired before or independently of C-Transit remain yours. What you learned specifically by reading C-Transit's code and plans does not.

## Who owns the work

Work created for C-Transit, using C-Transit resources, in the scope of your role, belongs to C-Transit. This applies to source code, firmware, designs, schematics, CAD files, documentation, databases, and written content.

If you brought background materials into the project — pre-existing code, templates, design components — those are disclosed separately in Schedule B of your physical agreement. You retain ownership of them, but you grant C-Transit a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free license to use them in connection with C-Transit's business.

The physical agreement includes the actual IP assignment. The digital acceptance here records your acknowledgment of the intent; the physical signature completes the legal transfer.

## When someone leaves

If you decide to stop contributing, or C-Transit decides to end the relationship, your accrued contributions are treated according to the departure rules in the physical agreement. The rules distinguish between four kinds of departure — resignation for a good reason, resignation for a bad reason, termination for a good reason, termination for a bad reason — because the Slicing Pie methodology treats them differently:

- If you leave for good reasons and honor your commitments, you keep all your accrued slices.
- If you leave for bad reasons — for example, breaking confidentiality, leaving mid-project without notice, or acting against C-Transit — some or all of your slices may return to the Pie.

Execution Tracker records the departure event. The physical agreement defines what happens next.

## Disputes

If you disagree with an admin decision — a rejected log, a disputed contribution, a departure categorization — the path is:

1. **Raise it directly** with the admin within 10 days of the decision. Most disagreements end here.
2. **Request a written review** if a conversation does not resolve it. The admin provides the specific reasons in writing.
3. **Escalate to mediation** if a written review does not resolve it. A mutually chosen independent adviser reviews the ledger and the evidence.
4. **Resort to Nigerian courts** if mediation fails.

Nothing in this process prevents you from asserting a legal right in a Nigerian court. Nothing prevents you from reporting a legal violation to the appropriate authority.
